# Evidence and scope

## Established literature basis

Published clocks support an early developmental decline in estimated epigenetic age. Human stage coverage and adult-clock calibration limit its interpretation. Neither complete repair of all inherited damage nor a universal zero age is established by these readings. A recent developmental regulatory-grammar theory is a hypothesis, not a proven single mechanism.

## Executed analyses

The public pilot analyses recovered fibroblast expression from one published study. It does not compute a DNA methylation clock, estimate lifespan, train an age predictor or infer gene-specific causal effects. There are only three independent donors. Experiment-holdout allows the same donors in training and testing. Marker removal is a narrow diagnostic, not comprehensive control of proliferation or pluripotency.

The synthetic benchmark defines its causal truth in the generator. The model's strong results on interaction mechanisms reflect a correctly specified predictor under randomized action assignment. These outcomes cannot nominate real genes. The clock-only decoy and absent-combination scenarios demonstrate failure modes. Policy evaluation uses an oracle identity constraint; this is explicitly easier than predicting biological identity and safety.

## Prospective components

Atlas integration, genomic regulatory-element localization, a biologically trained multimodal graph model, local stability estimation, functional confirmation, rescue experiments, tissue validation and longevity studies have not been completed. The graph reference passed finite-gradient checks and a three-step optimizer smoke test on invented tensors with PyTorch 2.5.1 CPU. It has not been trained on biological data. Its normalized continuous reconstruction is not a raw-count multiomic likelihood implementation.

## Required evidence before a mechanism claim

1. Assigned and independently verified interventions, with appropriate controls.
2. Replicated independent functional improvement toward an appropriate youthful reference.
3. Preserved tissue identity, acceptable viability and separately measured genomic integrity.
4. Necessity, sufficiency and rescue in a stated cell context.
5. Persistence after withdrawal and recovery from controlled challenge.
6. Donor and study replication, with multiplicity-controlled confirmation.
7. Direct cis-element experiments for any genomic switch claim.
8. Survival data for any lifespan claim.

No candidate currently meets those criteria in this release.
